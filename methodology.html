FormFactor Analyze Methodology

How FormFactor works

1. Finding your body

FormFactor uses Google's MediaPipe to find points on your body in every camera frame. It uses your shoulder, hip, knee, and ankle on the side closest to the camera. Everything runs in your browser, so your video never leaves your device.

2. What the research says

  • Rounding your lower back while lifting puts a lot of stress on your spinal discs. [1, 2]
  • A little back movement is normal, about 9-22° in the lower back. [3]
  • How much you lean forward depends on your leg length. About 15-45° is typical. [5, 6]
  • The real danger is your back angle changing fast in the middle of a lift. [6, 7]
  • Comparing how much your trunk and shins lean shows if your hips or knees did more of the work. [4]

3. How it decides

  • Red: your chest drops forward really fast.
  • Yellow: your hips rise before your chest, you lean more than 45° (squats), or a rep is a lot different from your first rep.
  • Green: none of those happened.

Which muscles worked more: trunk lean minus shin lean. More than +10° means your hips did more work, less than -10° means your knees did, and in between is even. [4]

4. Limits

  • One camera can't see the curve of your back, only a line from your shoulder to your hip.
  • It can't measure force or how much weight you're lifting.
  • It works best from the side, with your whole body in view and good lighting.
  • I tested it on 3 photos from a research paper [4] and it matched all 3, but it hasn't been tested in a lab.
